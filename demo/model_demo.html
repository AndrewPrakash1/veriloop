VeriLoop

A compliance-agent environment whose reward is computed from the database state an episode produced — not from what the agent says about it.

The claim, tested before any GPU time was spent: a reward that reads the agent's prose pays 1.000 to all ten of the shortcuts we could build. A reward that reads the database closes 10 of 10. The table in §2 is that measurement.

The base model does not merely choose the wrong tool — in 18 of 20 episodes it could not emit a parseable tool call at all. After SFT, every episode executes the full protocol and terminates, with no failure of any kind.

Why the 1.0 means something. It decomposes exactly as the reward specifies: 0.5 mandatory + 0.3 final_state + 0.2 efficiency. And that reward cannot be narrated around — see §2. A perfect score here is verified against the world, not against the agent's prose.

04What is not claimed

  • The 20 episodes are all kyc_clean_pass — the template that is 40 % of training. This shows SFT taught the protocol; it is not a robustness result. E2 (unseen templates, 60 % negatives) was not run.
  • The RL arms were not run. train/rl.py is implemented (RLOO over the arm's reward) and was probed on the GPU. The probe found the SFT policy saturates the training distribution (4 of 4 rollouts identical → every advantage exactly 0.0 → no gradient), and rollouts cost 56.75 s per 8-turn episode — ~3.8 h / ₹350 for a single 30-step arm. Reported as measured rather than replaced with a token run.
  • Single seed; no error bars on the SFT arm.
  • Negatives are 11 % of training examples but 60 % of E2/E3 — the model is trained mostly on the happy path.
